# Psalms 23 and 91 in Working Tradition: Hoodoo and Kabbalah

**Verified context:** Reference document for serious practitioner use. Lineage-based practice notes drawn from cited sources below.

---

## 1. Psalm 91 in Hoodoo Practice

Psalm 91 is the load-bearing protection text of the rootwork tradition — what practitioners commonly call "the ultimate protection spell, a spiritual bulletproof vest." Its presence in Hoodoo arrives largely through **Godfrey Selig's *Secrets of the Psalms: A Fragment of the Practical Kabala*** (Roman-Hebrew, German original 1788; English in the U.S. by Dorene Publishing in the late 1930s–40s), which catherine yronwode documents as the principal Jewish-Kabbalistic transmission line into African American conjure (Lucky Mojo, *"Secrets of the Psalms": The Kabbalist Influence on Hoodoo*).

The holy name assigned to Psalm 91 in Selig's Kabbalistic schema is **El Shaddai** ("God Almighty"). The instruction in that lineage is that the practitioner holds the divine name in mind, or pronounces it, before reciting the Psalm. The Psalm is then deployed against pestilence, enemies, dangers of the road, and witchcraft set against the home.

**Documented working structure (composite from Hoodoo teachers):**

- **Candle:** White most common, purple secondary, for protection. For an active "boundary around the household," a *seven-day white glass-encased candle* is typical, dressed top-down (toward you, for drawing protection in) with Protection, Fiery Wall of Protection, or "Can't Touch This" condition oil.
- **Dressing herbs at base:** Crushed rue, hyssop, agrimony, black salt, and red brick dust (for boundary and reversal). Hyssop is the canonical Biblical purification herb (Psalm 51:7) and is cross-traditional.
- **Petition:** Names of household members written on brown paper, crossed by the text of Psalm 91 written crosswise over the names.
- **Timing:** Recite **three times** at the lighting, then once daily until the candle finishes. Saturday (Saturn — boundary, restriction, walls) is the traditional protection day; Tuesday (Mars) when the work is reversal of an active enemy attack.
- **Application:** Anoint the body (crown, throat, wrists, ankles) and the four corners of the threshold (doorways, window frames) with the dressed oil while reciting verses 9–11.

(Sources: jesterbear.com/Hoodoo, Rich Bitch Conjure, Living Talismans, Lucky Mojo *Secrets of the Psalms*.)

## 2. Psalm 23 in Hoodoo Practice

Psalm 23 in Hoodoo is **the all-purpose blessing text** — used for prosperity, gambling luck, dream divination, journey safety, marital harmony, and as a closing/sealing psalm in nearly every working.

**Documented practices:**

- **Prosperity working:** Anoint with olive oil mixed with **bayberry oil**, recite Psalm 23 for **seven consecutive mornings** upon rising. (jesterbear.com/Hoodoo, sourced from Selig.)
- **Candle:** Green or gold candle for the prosperity reading; white for blessing/peace; light blue for marital harmony. Dress with Money Drawing, Good Fortune, or Bayberry oil.
- **Job's Tears working (yronwode):** On the seventh morning, hold seven Job's Tears seeds in the hand, walk to running water, recite Psalm 23, and throw the seeds over the **left shoulder** into the moving water to "lay the trick."
- **Dream divination:** Recite Psalm 23 immediately before sleep, asking that the answer be given in dream.
- **Travel:** Recite Psalm 23 the morning of a journey, "against all manner of bad luck."
- **Cup runneth over:** Verse 5 ("Thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over") is the line spoken aloud while dressing prosperity candles or feeding mojo bags.

(Sources: jesterbear.com/Hoodoo, Rich Bitch Conjure, Living Talismans.)

## 3. The Canonical Pairing — Psalm 23 + 91 (and 29)

The documented Hoodoo pairing is actually a **triad**: **Psalm 23 + Psalm 29 + Psalm 91**, recited together "to quiet a tumultuous or disorderly ghost" or to drive an oppressive spirit from the house, then forcefully ordering the entity to depart (jesterbear.com/Hoodoo, citing Selig).

The functional logic of the pairing:

- **Psalm 91** = the wall, the boundary, the active protection
- **Psalm 23** = the blessing within the wall, peace and provision in the protected space
- **Psalm 29** = the voice of the Lord that breaks the cedars — the active commanding force against the spirit

Common practitioner deployment: read **Psalm 91 first** to establish protection, then **Psalm 23 last** as the sealing blessing. Many rootworkers use this opening-and-closing structure around any working: 91 to open the perimeter, 23 to seal in the blessing. This is the basis for Hoodoo Sen Moise's emphasis on Psalms as the verbal armature of the work itself (*Working Conjure*, Weiser 2018).

## 4. Psalm 91 in Kabbalistic Tradition — Shimmush Tehillim

The **Sefer Shimmush Tehillim** ("On the Magical Uses of the Psalms"), a medieval Hebrew text of uncertain authorship (commonly dated 10th–11th century Geonic period, possibly earlier), is the source-stratum from which Selig later drew. It is the document that assigns each Psalm a divine Name, a magical purpose, and an instruction.

The Talmud (**Shevu'ot 15b**) already calls Psalm 91 **"Shir shel Pega'im"** — the *Song of Plagues* or *Song against Evil Spirits / Demons*. From the **Geonic period onward** it was recited to drive away demons; the Targum to Psalm 91 explicitly names the demons referenced. **Rashi** glosses *deber* ("pestilence," v. 6) and *qetev* ("destruction," v. 6) as the proper names of two demons; the "fall" of v. 7 (*yipol*) is read by Rashi as *camp* — eleven thousand demons camping at your side, from which the wings of Shekhinah shelter you.

The **Shimmush Tehillim** instruction for Psalm 91 (paired with Psalm 90, the preceding "Psalm of Moses"): recite both Psalms over a person tormented by an evil spirit or stricken with an incurable disease, while invoking **El Shaddai** in petition that He release the sufferer "for the sake of Your great, mighty, and holy Name."

## 5. Psalm 91 — Verse-Specific Protections (Talmudic, Midrashic, and Shimmush)

- **vv. 1–2** (*"He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High"*) — opening invocation, recited as the entry into the protected dwelling. Inscribed at the lintel of doorways in some Sephardic and Eastern traditions.
- **vv. 5–6** (Terror of night, arrow by day, *deber*, *qetev*) — the four classical demon categories. These verses are the explicit anti-demonic core; recited at twilight against **night-terrors** and in childbirth/postpartum rooms to guard mother and infant against **Lilith** and her hosts.
- **v. 7** (*"A thousand shall fall at thy side"*) — recited for protection in battle, in physical violence, and during epidemic (the Psalm's role during plague years is documented in both Jewish and Christian traditions).
- **vv. 9–10** (*"No evil shall befall thee, neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling"*) — household boundary verses; written on amulets affixed to the doorpost.
- **v. 11** (*"He shall give His angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways"*) — the **travel protection** verse, recited before journeys. The amulet tradition uses this verse as the central inscription on a **Kamea (k'mia)** worn around the neck while traveling.
- **vv. 14–16** (the divine first-person promises, *"Because he hath set his love upon Me…"*) — the closing seal; recited last, often seven times.

A widespread practice: recite verse 11 **three times** before stepping out of the house, and recite the entire Psalm seven times on the eve of any journey of more than a day's distance.

(Sources: Wikipedia *Psalm 91* citing Shevu'ot 15b, Rashi, and Targum; Living Talismans on Shimmush Tehillim; thetorah.com.)

## 6. Suppressed / Apocryphal Psalms (151–155)

- **Psalm 151** — canonical in **Eastern Orthodox**, **Ethiopian Tewahedo**, **Coptic**, **Armenian Apostolic**, and **Syriac** churches; Hebrew original recovered at Qumran in the **Great Psalms Scroll (11Q5)** as two separate compositions (151A and 151B). Used in the Ethiopian imperial coronation rite. No widely-published Hoodoo working tradition uses Psalm 151, but it appears in some African Diasporic Christian practice (Ethiopian, Caribbean) as a youth-blessing and warrior-anointing psalm, given David's anointing as a youth is its content.
- **Psalms 152–155** — preserved only in **Syriac manuscripts** (and partially at Qumran for 154 and 155). They are part of the *Five Apocryphal Psalms of David*. Used liturgically in Syriac Christianity; not part of standard Hoodoo working canon.
- **11QapocrPs (11Q11)** — the **"Four Songs Against Demons"** scroll from Qumran includes Psalm 91 alongside three otherwise-unknown psalms, used by the Qumran community as **exorcism texts**. This is the earliest documented Jewish exorcism-Psalter and confirms Psalm 91's anti-demonic function dates to at least the Second Temple period.

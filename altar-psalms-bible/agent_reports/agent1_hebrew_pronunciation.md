## 1. Psalm 91, Verses 1–2 — Hebrew, Transliteration, Meaning

### Verse 1

**Hebrew (with nikud):**
יֹשֵׁב בְּסֵתֶר עֶלְיוֹן בְּצֵל שַׁדַּי יִתְלוֹנָן

**Practical transliteration for English speakers:**
**yoh-SHEV b'-SEH-ter el-YOHN, b'-TZEL shah-DAI yit-loh-NAHN**

**Literal English:**
"He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall lodge in the shadow of the Almighty."

**Word-by-word:**

| Hebrew | Pronounce | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| יֹשֵׁב | **yoh-SHEV** | "dwelling / sitting" |
| בְּסֵתֶר | **b'-SEH-ter** | "in the secret / hidden place" |
| עֶלְיוֹן | **el-YOHN** | "the Most High" *(divine name)* |
| בְּצֵל | **b'-TZEL** | "in the shadow" (the *tz* is one sound, like the "ts" in "cats") |
| שַׁדַּי | **shah-DAI** | "the Almighty / All-Sufficient" *(divine name — rhymes with "shy")* |
| יִתְלוֹנָן | **yit-loh-NAHN** | "shall lodge / abide overnight" |

### Verse 2

**Hebrew (with nikud):**
אֹמַר לַיהוָה מַחְסִי וּמְצוּדָתִי אֱלֹהַי אֶבְטַח־בּוֹ

**Practical transliteration:**
**oh-MAR la-doh-NAI mahkh-SEE u-m'-tzu-da-TEE, eh-loh-HAI ev-TAKH-bo**

**Literal English:**
"I will say of the LORD, my refuge and my fortress; my God, in Him I trust."

**Word-by-word:**

| Hebrew | Pronounce | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| אֹמַר | **oh-MAR** | "I will say" |
| לַיהוָה | **la-doh-NAI** | "to the LORD" (the four-letter Name יהוה is *spoken* as "Adonai") |
| מַחְסִי | **mahkh-SEE** | "my refuge / shelter" (the *kh* is a throat-clearing sound, soft, like the *ch* in *Bach*) |
| וּמְצוּדָתִי | **u-m'-tzu-da-TEE** | "and my fortress" |
| אֱלֹהַי | **eh-loh-HAI** | "my God" (the *ai* ending = "eye") |
| אֶבְטַח־בּוֹ | **ev-TAKH-bo** | "I trust in Him" |

**Pronunciation notes for an English speaker:**
- **Stress** in Hebrew usually falls on the **last syllable** of a word. The CAPS in the transliteration show where to lean.
- **kh** (in *mahkh-see, ev-takh*) = a soft scrape in the back of the throat, like clearing a hair. Not a hard *k*. If you can't make the sound, a gentle *h* substitutes acceptably.
- **tz** = the "ts" sound in *boots*. One consonant cluster, not two syllables.
- **r** is rolled lightly at the back, but a regular English *r* is fine for ritual use.
- The **'** (apostrophe) in *b'-SEH-ter, b'-TZEL, u-m'-tzu* is the *sh'va* — a near-silent half-vowel, like the "uh" in *the* when said quickly: "buh-SEH-ter" said fast becomes "b'SEH-ter."

## 2. Divine Names in Psalm 91

Four sacred names appear in this psalm. Each carries a distinct working signature.

| Name | Hebrew | Transliteration | Meaning | How to speak it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **YHWH** (the Tetragrammaton) | יהוה | **Adonai** (ah-doh-NAI) | The unutterable Name; "I AM THAT I AM" — pure being, the Source | Jewish tradition never pronounces the four letters directly. Substitute **Adonai** ("my Lord") in working speech. For deep concentration on the Name itself, the practice is to *visualize* the four letters while speaking *Adonai*. |
| **Elohim** | אֱלֹהִים | **eh-loh-HEEM** | "God" — plural of majesty; the Name of judgment, structure, and law (the binding, ordering power) | *eh-loh-HEEM*, stress the last syllable. (In verse 2 it appears in possessive form: **Elohai** = "my God.") |
| **Elyon** | עֶלְיוֹן | **el-YOHN** | "The Most High" — the transcendent peak, that which is *above*. Used for cosmic supremacy and protection from above. | *el-YOHN.* Long *o*. Lean on the second syllable. |
| **Shaddai** | שַׁדַּי | **shah-DAI** | "The Almighty / All-Sufficient." Older tradition glosses it as *El who said "enough"* — the limit-setter, the sealer, the Name written on the back of every mezuzah. The shielding Name. | *shah-DAI*, rhymes with "shy." This is the Name on the doorpost — protection at the threshold. |

For Hoodoo working purposes: **Elyon** calls down from above, **Shaddai** seals the perimeter, **Adonai (YHWH)** consecrates, **Elohim/Elohai** binds the working in the order of law. Verse 1 invokes the first two in a single breath — the cosmic above and the protective shadow.

## 3. The Simple Chant — Verse 1 for the Altar

Repeat this at the candle, three or seven times, on the slow exhale:

> **yoh-SHEV b'-SEH-ter el-YOHN
> b'-TZEL shah-DAI yit-loh-NAHN**

Cadence: two long breaths. First breath: "yoh-SHEV b'SEH-ter el-YOHN." Second breath: "b'TZEL shah-DAI yit-loh-NAHN." Let the *el-YOHN* rise. Let the *yit-loh-NAHN* settle.

If you want a shorter seed-phrase to hold under the breath while dressing the candle or trimming the wick, use just the divine-name pair:

> **el-YOHN... shah-DAI...**

Above, and around. The Most High calls down; the Almighty closes the circle.

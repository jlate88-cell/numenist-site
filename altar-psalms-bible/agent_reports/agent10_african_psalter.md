## 1. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Canon (81 books)

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church holds the broadest biblical canon of any historically continuous Christian communion: **46 books in the Old Testament and 35 in the New, totaling 81.** It is not a "Catholic Bible plus extras." It is a parallel transmission that never passed through Rome.

What the Tewahedo canon preserves that the European canon dropped:

- **1 Enoch (Mäṣḥäfä Henok)** — in full, in Ge'ez. The book Jude quotes by name (Jude 1:14-15) survived complete *only* in Ethiopian transmission. The Latin and Greek churches had the citation in Jude but lost the source text for over a thousand years until James Bruce carried three Ge'ez manuscripts out of Ethiopia in 1773. The Aramaic Dead Sea Scroll fragments at Qumran confirmed in the 20th century that the Ethiopian text was a faithful copy of something the Mediterranean church had been blind to since late antiquity.
- **Jubilees (Mäṣḥäfä Kufale)** — the 49-year-cycle retelling of Genesis–Exodus, with the alternative solar calendar and the priestly chronology.
- **The three books of Mäqabyan** — *not* the Greek 1-2 Maccabees of the Septuagint. These are entirely distinct Ethiopian compositions, narrating different figures and themes. Same name, different books.
- **4 Baruch / Paralipomena of Jeremiah, Pseudo-Josephus (Joseph ben Gurion / Zena Ayhud)** — Ethiopian historical compositions tied to the broader Jewish-Christian milieu.
- **Mäs'hafä Kidan (Book of the Covenant / Testamentum Domini)** — Christ's post-resurrection teaching on church order, considered canonical NT in Ethiopia, unknown to Rome.
- **Senodos** — collected apostolic canons; **Didascalia** — the Ethiopic recension of apostolic church order; **Two Books of Clement** — letters attributed to Clement.

The Ethiopian list is at the Church's own site ([ethiopianorthodox.org](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html)) and is corroborated by [Wikipedia's Tewahedo canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Tewahedo_biblical_canon) and the academic survey by Anke Wanger ([EUCLID PDF](https://www.euclid.int/papers/Anke%20Wanger%20-%20Canon%20in%20the%20EOTC.pdf)).

## 2. The Coptic Psalter — Bohairic and Sahidic

The Egyptian church produced two parallel Psalter traditions in two Coptic dialects: **Sahidic** (Upper Egypt, Thebaid, dominant in the pre-Islamic period) and **Bohairic** (Lower Egypt, Alexandria/Delta, dominant after the 11th century and still the liturgical language of the Coptic Orthodox Church today). Both were translated from the **Septuagint** — the Greek Alexandrian recension — not from a Hebrew Masoretic original. That matters: the Coptic Psalter carries the Alexandrian text-tradition the Jewish diaspora of Egypt itself had produced.

**The Mudil Codex** (Coptic Museum Cairo, cat. 6614) is the load-bearing artifact. Discovered in 1984 in a Coptic cemetery at al-Mudil, about 45 km from ancient Oxyrhynchus, placed as a *pillow* beneath the head of an adolescent girl in her grave. 498 parchment folios, wooden boards, leather binding, written in the Middle Egyptian (Oxyrhynchite/Mesokemic) dialect — the oldest complete Coptic Psalter known, dated to the late 4th / early 5th century. The Sahidic Old Testament had been "systematically and completely translated" by the last quarter of the 4th century — the Mudil is contemporaneous with that translation horizon.

The significance: the Sahidic Psalter is uniquely valuable for Septuagint textual criticism because it descends from Greek manuscripts that **predate Origen's Hexapla** and the recensional corrections that reshaped the Greek text under Roman editorial pressure later. The Coptic Psalter is a window on what the Psalter looked like in Alexandria *before* the centralizing edits.

## 3. The Ge'ez Psalter — Mäzmurä Dawit

The Ethiopian Psalter, *Mäzmurä Dawit* (Psalms of David), is the densest single book in the Tewahedo liturgical life. Memorized whole by *dabtaras* and clergy. Its structure as it functions in Ethiopian practice:

- **151 Psalms** as canonical proper — Psalm 151 sits as canon, not as appendix. Emperor Haile Selassie I opened his first address to the Council of State by reciting Psalm 151 in full.
- **15 Biblical Canticles** (the Songs from the Old and New Testaments) — Moses, Miriam, Hannah, Habakkuk, the Magnificat, the Benedictus, the Nunc Dimittis, etc. — appended as standard.
- **Mahaleyä Mahalay (Song of Songs)** — Solomon's Song appended.
- **Wedasse Maryam (Praise of Mary)** — a Marian psalter organized by the seven days of the week; daily Marian devotion in psalm form.
- **Anqäṣä Berhan (Gate of Light)** — a second appended Marian psalter, sometimes paired with the Wedasse Wägnay.

**On Psalms 152–155:** these are the so-called "Five Syriac Psalms" preserved in the Syriac (Peshitta) tradition and partly attested at Qumran (11QPs^a). The Ge'ez Mazmura Dawit as it is standardly transmitted **does not** include 152-155; the Ethiopian extension stops at 151. The supplementary canticles, Song of Songs, and the two Marian psalters are what makes the Ethiopian Psalter a much larger devotional book than the Western "150 Psalms" — but the 152-155 set is Syriac, not Ge'ez.

## 4. The North African Fathers — Not "Influenced by Rome," Earlier than Rome

The systematizers of Christian doctrine were African before they were anything else. Geography is theology here.

- **Origen** (Alexandria, c. 185–253) — first systematic Christian theologian; the Hexapla, the *De Principiis*, the exegetical method the entire later tradition is downstream of.
- **Clement of Alexandria** (c. 150–215) — *Stromateis*; the synthesis with Hellenistic philosophy.
- **Athanasius** (Alexandria, c. 296–373) — Nicene Christology; and **the 39th Festal Letter of 367 CE**: the earliest document anywhere that lists the 27 books of the New Testament as we now have them. An *Egyptian Coptic bishop*, writing to fix the Easter date for his African churches, defines the New Testament. Not a Roman pope. Not a council in Italy. ([Christian History Institute](https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/athanasius-defines-new-testament), [New Advent: Letter 39](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm))
- **Cyril of Alexandria** (c. 376–444) — Christology that gave us the Theotokos formula at Ephesus.
- **Tertullian** (Carthage, c. 155–220) — the first major Latin Christian writer; he coined the Latin word *trinitas*. Latin Christian theology begins in Africa.
- **Cyprian** (Carthage, c. 200–258) — ecclesiology, episcopal unity.
- **Augustine** (Hippo Regius, modern Algeria, 354–430) — the most influential theologian in Western Christianity, full stop. African.

The pattern: the conceptual instruments Rome later inherited — Trinity, canon, Christology, the doctrinal vocabulary itself — were forged on African soil first.

## 5. The Garima Gospels — Africa's Documentary Receipt

Two Ge'ez gospel codices preserved at the Abba Garima monastery in Tigray, northern Ethiopia. **Radiocarbon dating at Oxford placed Garima 2 at approximately 390–570 CE and Garima 1 at approximately 530–660 CE.** Before this testing, Western scholars assumed they were medieval — about 900 years old. The actual dates make them **contemporary with or older than Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus**, the great 4th-century Greek codices that anchor Western text-critical scholarship.

Garima 2 is a candidate for the **earliest surviving complete illuminated Christian manuscript on earth**. Illuminated. With evangelist portraits, canon tables, decorated headpieces. African Christians were producing fully illustrated bound Gospel books before the Lindisfarne Gospels were even conceived — by at least 200 years.

The Garima Gospels are documentary proof, not theory: Africa had a full, mature, *artistic* Christian textual tradition before the Roman conciliar machinery that supposedly "produced" the Bible had finished its work.

## 6. Beta Israel — The Ethiopian Jewish Psalter

The Beta Israel ("House of Israel"; *Falasha* is exonymic and pejorative — "exile / stranger") are the Jewish community of northern Ethiopia. Their own tradition traces lineage to the Solomonic line through Menelik I, son of the Queen of Sheba — a continuous Jewish identity in the Horn of Africa that **did not pass through the rabbinic Mishnaic synthesis** of the Mediterranean diaspora. They do not use the Talmud. They preserve a pre-rabbinic, Second-Temple-era Judaism.

- **Sacred text:** the *Orit* — an **Octateuch**: the five books of Moses *plus* Joshua, Judges, and Ruth — read liturgically in Ge'ez, not Hebrew. (Hebrew enters Beta Israel practice only after the late-20th-century migration to Israel.)
- **Wider scriptures:** the *Mäṣḥafä Kedus* — the broader sacred library — includes a Psalter and other writings overlapping with the Tewahedo broader canon. Their priestly transmission line (the *qessoch*) was monastic and copied texts to preserve them through persecution.
- **Protective psalmody:** the Beta Israel use the Psalms liturgically and apotropaically. The community preserved practices including *seged*, fasting, ritual purity laws, and the use of sacred recitation for protection. The protective use of Psalm 91 and the road-opening function of Psalm 23 in Black diaspora rootwork has a structural cousin in the Ethiopian Jewish protective psalter tradition — both inherit from a Second-Temple Semitic stratum where psalms were *operative*, not merely devotional. (Western Ashkenazi tradition by contrast displaced operative psalmody into rabbinic legal study; the African retention is older.)

## 7. The Lineage Claim — Documentable, Not Romantic

**Thomas C. Oden**, *How Africa Shaped the Christian Mind: Rediscovering the African Seedbed of Western Christianity* (IVP, 2007). Oden — a white Methodist theologian, former liberal turned paleo-orthodox — argues seven specific dependencies of Western Christianity on Africa: (1) the Western university concept; (2) Christian scriptural exegesis; (3) the substance of early Christian doctrine (Tertullian, Origen, Cyprian, Athanasius, Augustine); (4) conciliar/ecumenical decision-making patterns; (5) Christian monasticism; (6) Christian Neoplatonism; (7) rhetorical and dialectical method. His thesis: *"The pattern is not from north to south from Europe to Africa, but the other way around."* ([InterVarsity Press page](https://www.ivpress.com/how-africa-shaped-the-christian-mind))

**John S. Mbiti** (1931–2019), Kenyan Anglican philosopher, *African Religions and Philosophy* (Heinemann, 1969). The foundational work establishing that African religious cognition is not a primitive substrate awaiting Christian arrival but a sophisticated ontology with which Christianity *encountered* and from which African Christianity emerged with its own dignity. Mbiti's argument against "imported Christianity" is the methodological frame for treating African Christian transmission as a peer, not a derivative. ([Wikipedia: John Mbiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mbiti))

**Cyril Okorocha** (Nigerian Anglican theologian) and the wider African theological school built on Mbiti and Oden continue the project: the Christian textual and conceptual tradition has African roots that predate and shape its European systematization.

The claim is not Afrocentrism. It is geography plus dates: Origen was teaching in Alexandria when Rome had bishops barely literate in Greek; Tertullian was forging Latin theological vocabulary while the Roman church was still administratively a Greek-speaking immigrant community.

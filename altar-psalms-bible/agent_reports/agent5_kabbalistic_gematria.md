# Psalm 91: The Deep Kabbalistic Layer

**Reference document for Hermetic-Hoodoo-Kabbalistic practice.**

---

## 1. Sefer Shimush Tehillim — "The Magical Use of the Psalms"

The *Sefer Shimush Tehillim* is a medieval Hebrew manual (printed Italy 1551, but circulating in manuscript centuries earlier) that catalogues each Psalm's working application, the divine name that activates it, and the operational context. Its opening principle is the operative axiom of the whole tradition: *"The entire Torah is composed of the names of God, and in consequence it has the property of saving and protecting man."* Each Psalm is therefore a structured emission of the Name — the working consists of pronouncing it with intention, often paired with a specific divine name and a material substrate (water, oil, parchment).

For **Psalm 91 (Tehilla 91, *Yoshev B'seter Elyon*)** the Shimush tradition prescribes it as the master psalm for:

- **Protection from harmful spirits, demons (*mazikin*), and the "evil eye"** — recited over the threshold, on amulets (*kameot*), and worn on the body.
- **Protection of travelers** — recited before a journey, especially at night.
- **Exorcism and the lifting of plague** — paired with the names *Shaddai* and *Elyon*. The Dead Sea Scroll 11Q11 (11QapocrPs) already classifies Psalm 91 among the "Four Psalms Against Demons" used by the Qumran community for exorcism — placing the operational use back to at least the 1st century BCE.
- **Protection of the newborn and the dying** — written on parchment, placed under the pillow.

The associated divine names in the working tradition are **Shaddai** (*the name on the mezuzah*, the protective shield-name) and **YHWH** in its full vocalization, with the angelic guardians of v.11 (*"He shall give His angels charge over you"*) — Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, Raphael — invoked at the four cardinal points around the operator.

(Sources: Living Talismans on Shimush Tehillim; sacred-texts.com *Jewish Magic and Superstition* §8.)

## 2. The 72 Names (Shem HaMephorash) and Psalm 91

The **72-letter Name** is *not* derived from Psalm 91. It is constructed boustrophedonically from **Exodus 14:19–21** — three verses of exactly 72 letters each, read right-left-right, producing 72 three-letter triads (Rashi; *Sefer HaBahir*). It is the name Moses used to part the Red Sea.

In **practical Kabbalah**, however, several of the 72 triads are paired with Psalm 91 in protection work:

- **ל·ל·ה (Lelahel)** — name #6, for healing and protection from spiritual sickness.
- **כ·ל·י (Kaliel)** — name #19, judgment and protection from false accusation.
- **מ·ל·ה (Mileh)** — for protection in danger.
- **ה·ה·ה (Hahahel)** — protection of the spiritual mission.

Aryeh Kaplan, in *Meditation and Kabbalah*, treats the 72 Names as meditative gateways rather than incantations — the practitioner *enters* the Name rather than wielding it. When paired with Psalm 91, the Name is held in the mind while the Psalm is voiced; the Psalm provides the protective vessel, the Name provides the charge.

(Source: Wikipedia *Shem HaMephorash*; Kaplan, *Meditation and Kabbalah*, archive.org.)

## 3. The Divine Names Inside Psalm 91

The opening two verses contain **four distinct Names** in eleven Hebrew words — a density unmatched in the Psalter. This is the structural fact the Kabbalists fixate on.

- **עֶלְיוֹן (Elyon) — "Most High."** Sefirotically attributed to *Keter* (the supernal crown) or its outward face, *Arikh Anpin* (the "Long Face," the patient unmanifest). The shelter (*seter*) of Elyon is the concealed crown — the place above the angels of destruction. The Zohar identifies *seter Elyon* with the hidden upper sanctuary the soul retreats to in moments of mortal danger.

- **שַׁדַּי (Shaddai) — "the Sufficient / Almighty."** The name engraved on the *mezuzah* and the *tefillin* — the name of *boundary* and *protection*. Sefirotically *Yesod* (the foundation, the channel through which the upper worlds enter the lower). Read as acronym: *Shomer Daltot Yisrael* — "Guardian of the Doors of Israel." The "shadow of Shaddai" (*b'tzel Shaddai*) is the protective penumbra cast by the boundary-keeping Name.

- **יהוה (YHWH) — the Tetragrammaton.** *Tiferet*, the heart of the Tree, the harmonizing center. The unutterable, the four letters that contain all four worlds (Atzilut–Beriah–Yetzirah–Asiyah, one letter each).

- **אֱלֹהִים (Elohim).** Sefirotically *Gevurah* (judgment/severity) and *Binah* (understanding). The plural form is the Name of measured power — judgment held in trust.

**Why verse 2 is operationally exceptional:** *"I will say of YHWH: He is my refuge and my fortress, my Elohim in whom I trust"* — the practitioner declares YHWH (mercy/Tiferet) and Elohim (judgment/Gevurah) *as one name on the same breath*. This is the unification (*yichud*) of Mercy and Judgment, the central act of Kabbalistic prayer. The four Names in verses 1–2 traverse the Tree from Keter down through Tiferet, Gevurah, and Yesod — a complete vertical descent of the protective current. The reciter is not naming God four times; the reciter is wiring a circuit.

(Source: Yeshivat Har Etzion, *Tehillim 91 - A Song of Afflictions*; Wikipedia *Psalm 91* on Machzor Vitry; Kaplan *Meditation and Kabbalah*.)

## 4. Encoded Structure: Verse 16 Doubled, and the Letter-Pattern

Psalm 91 is *not* an alphabetic acrostic. But it carries two encoded structures the tradition treats as load-bearing:

**The doubled final verse.** Per *Machzor Vitry* (11th c., school of Rashi), verse 16 — *Orech yamim asbi'ehu, v'arehu bishu'ati* ("With length of days I will satisfy him, and show him My salvation") — is recited *twice* at the close, in Pesukei DeZimra of Shabbat and in the bedtime liturgy. The reason given: *the doubling completes the spelling of a Name of God.* The two recitations together yield the letters that finish a divine name initiated earlier in the Psalm — making the Psalm itself a graphic sigil that only resolves when spoken twice.

**The "Song of Plagues" (*Shir Shel Pega'im*).** Talmud *Shevuot* 15b records Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi calling Psalm 91 by two near-identical titles: *shir shel pega'im* (song against demonic strikes) and *shir shel nega'im* (song against plagues). A one-letter change (*pe* → *nun*) flips the operative field from spirits to disease — the same psalm operates on both registers because the structure underneath is identical. The Talmud reports R. Yehoshua ben Levi *would not recite it as an incantation in public, lest one rely on the verse and not on God,* but he recited it privately every night before sleep.

(Sources: Wikipedia *Psalm 91*; My Jewish Learning, *Shevuot 15*; Talmudology on Shevuot 15b.)

## 5. *Yoshev B'seter Elyon* — The Three Operative Contexts

**Before sleep.** Sleep is, in Kabbalistic anthropology, a 1/60th experience of death — the upper soul (*neshamah*) ascends and the lower soul (*nefesh*) remains in the body, exposed. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's nightly recitation of Psalm 91 is the source of the practice. In the *Kriyat Shema al ha-Mitah* (Bedtime Shema), Psalm 91 is the protective canopy over the sleeping body — the *seter Elyon* literally becomes the bedroom.

**After a funeral / accompanying the casket.** The psalm is recited **seven times** while accompanying the casket from the hearse to the grave. The seven recitations correspond to the seven *sefirot* of construction (*Chesed* through *Malkhut*) — the soul being escorted, name by name, through the seven gates of release. The Hoodoo parallel — graveyard dirt as a substance of crossing-over — sits in the same register: Psalm 91 marks the boundary between the worlds and bonds the spirit to safe passage.

**On a journey.** *Tefillat HaDerekh* (the wayfarer's prayer) is paired with or substituted by Psalm 91. The "way" in Kabbalistic reading is always two ways at once — the geographic road and the soul's road. Reciting *Yoshev B'seter* before leaving the threshold seals the *seter* (concealed shelter) around the traveler so the road cannot reach them through the senses while they are vulnerable in motion.

**Authorship:** *Midrash Tehillim* and the *Zohar* attribute Psalm 91 to **Moses**, composed either on the day he completed the Tabernacle or *while ascending Sinai through the cloud of the angels of destruction* — the protective utterance Moses voiced to pass through the wrathful host and reach the Throne. David later compiled it into the Psalter. This authorship matters operationally: the Psalm is in the lineage of the *one who crossed the threshold and returned*, which is why it is the threshold-text for sleep, death, and travel.

(Sources: Chabad.org *Psalm 91 – Yoshev B'Seter*; Sinai Memorial Chapel *Traditional Readings*; Sefaria *Midrash Tehillim 91*.)

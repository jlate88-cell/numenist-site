# Psalm 23 — The Shepherd Psalm in Five Voices

*A working-quality reference document for the Hoodoo-Hermetic-Kabbalistic practitioner. Texts are quoted as published; no harmonizing. Differences between renderings are themselves operative information.*

---

## 1. Hebrew Text — Verses 1-2 (Masoretic)

**Verse 1:**

מִזְמ֥וֹר לְדָוִ֑ד יְהוָ֥ה רֹ֝עִ֗י לֹ֣א אֶחְסָֽר׃

*Mizmor le-David. YHWH ro'i, lo echsar.*
"A psalm of David. YHWH (is) my shepherd; I shall not lack."

**Verse 2:**

בִּנְא֣וֹת דֶּ֭שֶׁא יַרְבִּיצֵ֑נִי עַל־מֵ֖י מְנֻח֣וֹת יְנַהֲלֵֽנִי׃

*Bin'ot deshe yarbitzeini; al-mei menuchot yenahaleini.*
"In meadows of fresh grass He makes me lie down; beside waters of restfulness He leads me."

The Tetragrammaton (יהוה) appears unvocalized — never pronounced in traditional reading, voiced as *Adonai* or *Hashem*. Direct invocation of YHWH as personal shepherd, first word a name. *Ro'i* is possessive — "my shepherd" — a single Hebrew word; the relational claim is grammatically inseparable from the act of naming. *Lo echsar* is one terse rebuttal of want — *echsar* from the root ח-ס-ר, "to lack, be deficient."

## 2. The Translations Side by Side

### Verse 1 — "The LORD is my shepherd"

| Source | Rendering |
|---|---|
| **Robert Alter (2007)** | "A David psalm. The LORD is my shepherd, I shall not want." |
| **Geneva Bible (1599)** | "A Psalme of David. The Lord is my shepheard, I shall not want." |
| **JPS Tanakh (1985)** | "The LORD is my shepherd; I lack nothing." |
| **NETS / Brenton LXX (Ps 22)** | "The Lord tends me as a shepherd, and I shall want nothing." |
| **KJV (1611)** | "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." |

*Note the Greek (LXX) verbalizes the noun: ποιμαίνει με κύριος — "the Lord shepherds me" — an active, ongoing tending rather than a static title. The Hermetic operator should hear: not "the Shepherd-One who is mine," but "the One who is shepherding me — now, in the act."*

### Verse 3 — The pivot

| Source | Rendering |
|---|---|
| **Alter** | "My life He brings back. He leads me on pathways of justice for His name's sake." |
| **Geneva 1599** | "He restoreth my soule, and leadeth me in the paths of righteousnesse for his Names sake." |
| **JPS 1985** | "He renews my life; He guides me in right paths as befits His name." |
| **LXX (Brenton)** | "He has restored my soul: he has guided me into the paths of righteousness, for his name's sake." |

*Critical word: נֶפֶשׁ (nefesh). Alter refuses "soul" — the Hebrew has no Platonic body/soul split. Nefesh is throat, breath, life-force, the living animal self. "My life He brings back" preserves the somatic charge the Christianized "restoreth my soule" laundered out. JPS splits the difference with "renews my life." For working purposes, **nefesh is the living breath, not a disembodied soul** — this matters when the psalm is being used to call something back into a body.*

### Verse 5 — Table, anointing, cup

| Source | Rendering |
|---|---|
| **Alter** | "You set out a table before me in the face of my foes. You moisten my head with oil, my cup overflows." |
| **Geneva 1599** | "Thou doest prepare a table before me in the sight of mine aduersaries: thou doest anoynt mine head with oyle, and my cuppe runneth ouer." |
| **JPS 1985** | "You spread a table for me in full view of my enemies; You anoint my head with oil; my drink is abundant." |
| **LXX (Brenton)** | "Thou hast prepared a table before me in presence of them that afflict me: thou hast thoroughly anointed my head with oil; and thy cup cheers me like the best wine." |

*The LXX adds the inebriating cup — μεθύσκον (methyskon), "intoxicating" — a sacramental drunkenness that the Masoretic merely calls "overflowing" (רְוָיָה / revayah, "saturation"). For a Hoodoo working with anointing, the LXX reading sanctions the cup as actively transformative, not merely full.*

### Verse 6 — The dwelling

| Source | Rendering |
|---|---|
| **Alter** | "Let but goodness and kindness pursue me all the days of my life. And I shall dwell in the house of the LORD for many long days." |
| **Geneva 1599** | "Doubtlesse kindnesse and mercie shall follow me all the dayes of my life, and I shall remaine a long season in the house of the Lord." |
| **JPS 1985** | "Only goodness and steadfast love shall pursue me all the days of my life, and I shall dwell in the house of the LORD for many long years." |
| **LXX (Brenton)** | "Thy mercy also shall follow me all the days of my life: and my dwelling shall be in the house of the Lord for a very long time." |
| **KJV** | "Surely goodnesse and mercie shall follow me all the dayes of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for euer." |

*The Hebrew verb is יִרְדְּפוּנִי (yirdefuni) — to **pursue, chase, hunt down**. Goodness and chesed (covenantal loyalty/lovingkindness) are not strolling behind — they are running you down like hounds. Alter and JPS preserve this. Geneva and KJV soften to "follow." The hunt is operatively important: this is not passive grace, it is grace as predator. And the Hebrew never says "for ever" — it says le-orekh yamim, "for length of days." The KJV's "for euer" is a Christianized eternalization the Hebrew does not warrant; JPS and Alter restore the measured, earthly reading.*

## 3. Tzalmavet — What the Hebrew Actually Says

The pivot of the psalm is גֵּיא צַלְמָוֶת (*gei tzalmavet*) in v. 4.

- *gei* (גֵּיא) — a deep ravine, gorge, gully. Not a gentle "valley" — a steep cut between heights where light fails before noon. Same word as *Gei Hinnom* (Gehenna), the burning ravine outside Jerusalem.
- *tzalmavet* (צַלְמָוֶת) — Strong's H6757. **Two readings, both ancient, both operative:**
  1. **Compound:** צֵל (tzel, "shadow") + מָוֶת (mavet, "death") → "shadow-of-death" / "death-shadow"
  2. **Re-pointing:** *tzalmut* — from a root meaning the densest possible darkness, *the place where light fails entirely*. Many modern scholars (and JPS, with "deepest darkness") favor this reading because the word appears 17–18 times in the Tanakh (concentrated in Job) almost always paired with imagery of pure dark, not necessarily death.

**What the literal Hebrew says:** "the gorge of death-shadow" or "the gorge of deepest dark." Not a metaphor for dying. A geographic and ontological location — *the place where light cannot reach*. This is the only place in all Hebrew scripture where someone *walks through* tzalmavet and is not destroyed by it. That is the operative claim of the psalm: passage through annihilating dark with the named One present (*ki attah immadi* — "for You are with me," in the second person — see below).

## 4. Untranslatable Features of the Hebrew

**Rhythm.** The Hebrew is dense and stressed — each verse is a balanced bicolon (two parallel half-lines). Read aloud in Hebrew, it is roughly 55 words; the KJV inflates to 122. The compression is part of the working. Slow it down and you lose the spell.

**The pronoun shift.** This is the most important structural feature for ritual use. The psalm opens in **third person** — *He* makes me lie down, *He* leads me, *He* restores. At v. 4, where the speaker enters the death-ravine, it shifts to **second person** — *You* are with me, *Your* rod, *Your* staff, *You* prepare, *You* anoint. The shift happens precisely at the moment of greatest darkness. The grammar enacts what the words describe: in the bright pastures one *talks about* the Shepherd; in the dark gorge one *talks to* Him. The vocative is born in the dark. v. 6 returns partially to third person ("the house of YHWH"), but the address has been opened. Geneva, KJV, JPS, Alter all preserve this shift — but **none of them flag it**. It must be read aloud to be felt.

**Dual structure.** Verses 1-3 are pastoral — green meadows, still waters, restored breath. Verses 5-6 are royal/cultic — table, anointing, oil, cup, house. Verse 4 is the hinge — the ravine. The psalm is shaped as a passage from shepherd's pasture, through the dark cut, to the king's hall. Sheep at the start, anointed guest at the end. The transformation happens in the dark.

**Chesed (חֶסֶד) in v. 6** has no English equivalent. JPS's "steadfast love" is the standard scholarly try. It denotes loyalty within a covenant — the love that is bound by oath, not the love that is felt as sentiment. For working purposes: *chesed is the love that keeps its word*.

## 5. Practical Note for the Working

The Geneva 1599 is the version closest in time to the candle-and-petition lineage as it would have been practiced by English-speaking root workers before the King James (1611) became dominant. It is also pre-Jacobean — that is, untouched by the political theology that shaped the KJV under James I. For a Hoodoo lighting per the older lineage, Geneva is the lineage-faithful English.

For the operative Hebrew read — where *nefesh* is breath not soul, *gei tzalmavet* is the lightless ravine, *yirdefuni* is the hunt of grace, and *le-orekh yamim* is the length of days rather than abstract eternity — Alter and JPS together give the cleanest pre-Christianized English. Read the Hebrew first, the Geneva second, Alter or JPS third for sense, and ignore the KJV's softenings.
